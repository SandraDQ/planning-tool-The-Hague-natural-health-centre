## So what are tissue salts?

In 1873 a German doctor, Wilhelm Heinrich Schuessler, was fascinated by why people fall ill with particular illnesses. He determined that often, illness begins in the cell, and every cell needs a handful of minerals to work well. When a cell lacks a mineral, or can't use it properly, symptoms follow. So he developed these tissue salts, sometimes also called cell salts because they work on a cellular level to restore balance.

He chose 12 mineral salts that occur naturally in the body, with the aim of keeping them simple, so people could easily use them on their own. Tissue salts are made by grinding minute doses of the mineral with lactose until extremely fine, and then pressed into small tablets that melt under the tongue. Like homeopathic remedies, they are diluted step by step, so that the body receives the energy of the mineral and just teeny microscopic doses of the actual mineral. Think of tissue salts as _energy versions_ of our electrolytes: they don't replace the minerals in your food, they help your body **use** the minerals properly. For those of you who are lactose intolerant, tissue salts can also be found in alcohol or sucruse based forms.

## Three minerals up close

### Calcium: the builder of our structure

White chalk cliffs, marble and limestone are all calcium. In you, 99% of it sits in your bones and teeth, giving the body its structure. Yet bones are constantly being rebuilt, and wherever there's a wound, calcium starts the repair. It also helps blood clot, and every heartbeat and nerve impulse depends on it. Running low can show up as brittle bones, cramps or dental problems.

### Magnesium: the great relaxer

Magnesium sits at the heart of chlorophyll, catching sunlight in every green leaf. In the body it helps more than 300 enzymes do their work. It works closely with calcium: think of their relationship as yin and yang. While calcium makes muscles contract, magnesium lets them relax. Stress is a magnesium thief; cramps, twitching eyelids, sleeplessness, anxiety and tiredness can all be signs you're short.

### Sulphur: the protein builder

Sulphur is the yellow crystal of volcanoes and hot springs, and the reason garlic and onions have their bite. In the body it lets proteins form: every strand of hair is made of keratin, a sulphur-rich protein, and it keeps skin and nails strong and helps antibodies recognise germs. Most people don't think much about sulphur, but it is an essential mineral that also helps move fluids throughout the body, helping it to detox.

This is when the minerals are needed the most - during illness in order to support the body in its process to heal itself. ## Easy tips for every day

Tissue salts help your body _process_ minerals; food _supplies_ them but we need them **both** , the actual mineral via food or supplements and the energy of it. You can find calcium in yogurt, sardines and kale; magnesium in leafy greens, pumpkin seeds and dark chocolate while sulphur can be found in garlic, onions and eggs. Please remember that tissue salts provide a gentle energetic boost that can be extremley beneficial to the body, however, the bodies are still composed of matter that actual require the actual mineral.
